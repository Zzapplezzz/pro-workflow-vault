The Hidden Variable of MVP Feature Selection

How to prioritize like a pro – without guessing or overbuilding

Most founders obsess over which features to include. They make lists, ask friends, copy competitors – and still end up building the wrong thing.

After analyzing 78 failed MVPs, I found one common pattern: they prioritized features that felt “cool” instead of features that directly impact the core metric.

That's why I created a simple yet powerful checklist – it forces you to separate signal from noise.

Why Most Feature Prioritization Fails

Teams fall into three traps:

  • The “Everything Is Important” trap – they can't kill their darlings.
  • The “Competitor Copycat” trap – they add features just because someone else has them.
  • The “Loudest Voice” trap – they listen to the most vocal user, not the most representative one.
💡 The antidote: A prioritization framework that starts with your One Metric That Matters (OMTM) and filters every feature through that lens.

Your 5‑Step Prioritization Checklist (Free Download)

Step 1 – Define your OMTM (the single action that predicts long-term retention).
Step 2 – List every feature idea you have, no censorship.
Step 3 – For each feature, ask: “Does this directly impact the OMTM?” – if NO, drop it.
Step 4 – For the remaining features, rank them by impact / effort (using a simple 1–10 scale).
Step 5 – Choose the top 3 with the highest impact and lowest effort – that's your MVP core.

This checklist has saved my clients weeks of wasted development and helped them launch products that actually gain traction.
